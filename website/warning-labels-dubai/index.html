About warning labels

Warning labels are the smallest part of a safety system and often the most handled. Unlike wall-mounted safety signs, they sit on the hazard itself: a 'Danger, high voltage' label on a distribution board door, a hot-surface triangle on a steam pipe, a pinch-point label beside a conveyor roller. We produce warning labels in Dubai for facility teams, contractors, equipment suppliers and clinics.

Because labels live on metal, painted and plastic surfaces that get wiped, oiled or warmed, the material matters as much as the print. Polyester with a permanent acrylic adhesive is the usual choice for panels and machinery; vinyl suits curved or textured surfaces; polypropylene works for drums and containers. An overlaminate adds resistance to cleaning chemicals and abrasion.

Content comes from the people responsible for the equipment. Chemical hazard pictograms and statements should follow the supplier's safety data sheet, electrical labels the details from your engineer, and clinical labels such as 'Oxygen in use' or sharps notices the wording set by your facility. We print exactly what is supplied and proof every design first.

Materials

Polyester (PET)

A stiff, dimensionally stable film that handles heat, oil and wiping. The first choice for electrical panels, machinery and rating plates.

Vinyl

A softer film that conforms to curves, rivets and lightly textured surfaces such as pipes, guards and plastic housings.

Polypropylene

A tough, moisture-resistant film for chemical drums, jerrycans and storage bins where labels meet spills and handling.

Paper labels

Only for dry, short-term indoor uses such as temporary maintenance notices or equipment being moved.

Finishing options

Gloss or matte overlaminate

Seals the print against abrasion and cleaning agents; matte reduces glare on shiny panel doors.

Chemical-resistant laminate

A heavier protective layer for labels in workshops and stores where solvents or oils are handled.

Kiss-cutting

Labels are cut through the face only, so they peel cleanly from the sheet or roll during application.

Sequential numbering

Each label carries a unique number or barcode for asset and inspection tracking.

Where it’s used

  • Electrical distribution boards and panels
  • Machine guards, rollers and moving parts
  • Chemical containers and storage cabinets
  • Hot pipes, boilers and steam lines
  • Clinic oxygen points and sharps areas
  • Forklifts, generators and compressors

Artwork checklist

  • Supply wording and pictograms exactly as they appear in the data sheet or engineer's instructions
  • Send vector artwork; small text in low-resolution images blurs at label sizes
  • Keep text at 6 pt or larger and at least 1.5 mm from the cut line
  • State the surface type, heat exposure and any chemicals the label will meet
  • For numbered labels, give the starting number, format and any prefix

Warning labels FAQs

What is the difference between warning labels and safety signs?

Safety signs are rigid or vinyl signs fixed to walls, doors and posts to warn or instruct people in an area. Warning labels are small adhesive labels applied to the equipment or container itself. Most facilities need both: a sign on the plant room door, and labels on the panels and machines inside it.

Will the labels stay on in heat and outdoors?

Polyester or vinyl with a permanent adhesive and a protective laminate is the usual choice for outdoor and warm surfaces, and it resists fading far better than paper. How long a label stays in good condition depends on sun exposure, heat, cleaning and the surface it is on. Tell us the conditions and we will suggest a suitable combination.

Can you print chemical hazard labels?

We can print labels using the hazard pictograms, signal words and statements that your supplier's safety data sheet or your HSE team specifies. We do not classify chemicals or decide label content. Send the relevant information from the data sheet along with the container size, and we will lay out the label for your review.

Do you supply labels on rolls for dispensers?

Yes. Tell us the core diameter, maximum roll diameter, label orientation and gap your dispenser or applicator needs, and we will set up the roll to match. For hand application on site, kiss-cut sheets are often easier to carry and apply one at a time.

Can labels carry asset numbers or QR codes?

Yes. Variable data printing lets each label carry a unique number, barcode or QR code alongside the warning content, which helps maintenance teams link a label to an inspection record. Supply the data in a spreadsheet and we will proof samples from the start, middle and end of the sequence before the full run.
