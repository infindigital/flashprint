350 gsm vs 400 gsm Business Cards Compared

What the gsm figure really tells you, how 350 and 400 gsm cards differ in the hand and with finishes, and when each weight makes sense.

By the Flash Print Solution team14 September 20265 min read

In this article

  1. What gsm actually measures
  2. 350 gsm vs 400 gsm at a glance
  3. How finishes change the comparison
  4. When 350 gsm is the right call
  5. When 400 gsm earns its place
  6. Beyond 400 gsm
  7. How to decide with confidence
  8. FAQs

On a quote, 350 gsm and 400 gsm look close enough to be interchangeable. In the hand, the difference is noticeable: one feels like a solid, dependable card, while the other has a firmer snap that many people associate with a more considered brand. This comparison of 350 gsm vs 400 gsm business cards explains what the numbers mean, how each weight behaves with finishes, and which one suits your purpose.

What gsm actually measures

Gsm stands for grams per square metre. It is the weight of a single sheet of that paper measuring one metre by one metre, and it tells you how much material is in the board. A 400 gsm board contains roughly one-seventh more fibre than a 350 gsm board of the same type, which is why it feels stiffer and more substantial.

What gsm does not tell you directly is thickness. Printers call thickness caliper, and it depends on how the board is made. Coated boards are compressed and sealed with a mineral layer, so they are thinner for their weight. Uncoated and cotton boards are bulkier, so a 350 gsm uncoated card can feel as thick as, or thicker than, a 400 gsm coated one. When comparing quotes, always check that both weights refer to the same type of stock.

Quotes may also describe coated board simply as "art card" or "silk card". Ask for the gsm, the surface and whether lamination is included, so you are comparing like with like. Print quality itself does not change with weight: both boards take the same inks and give the same sharpness, because the surface, not the thickness, decides how the print looks.

How finishes change the comparison

Finishes often matter more than the base weight. Lamination adds a thin plastic film to one or both sides, which increases stiffness noticeably. A 350 gsm card with matte or soft-touch lamination on both sides can feel close to an unlaminated 400 gsm card, and for many businesses that combination offers the best balance of feel, durability and cost.

Embossing and debossing push the design into the board, so thickness gives the impression somewhere to go. A 400 gsm board, or a softer cotton stock, holds a deeper and cleaner impression than a thinner card. Foil stamping works well on both weights; the stock surface matters more than the weight for foil clarity, as our gold foil business cards demonstrate.

Heavier boards also demand more care in cutting and creasing. Folded business cards or cards with die-cut shapes are perfectly possible at 400 gsm, but they need proper scoring to fold cleanly without cracking the surface.

When 350 gsm is the right call

  • Large teams and frequent reorders. Where dozens of staff need cards and details change often, the standard weight keeps each reorder straightforward.
  • Laminated designs. If you plan to laminate, 350 gsm already gains plenty of stiffness.
  • Exhibitions and events. When cards are handed out in quantity, the lighter board is easier to carry and store.
  • Colourful, busy designs. When the print carries the impression, the board does not need to do all the work.
  • Cards that travel in packs. Cards inserted into welcome packs, folders or mailers sit flatter and add less bulk at the standard weight.

When 400 gsm earns its place

  • Minimal, typographic designs. A clean card with lots of white space relies on the feel of the board to communicate quality.
  • Unlaminated stocks. If you want a natural, uncoated finish without lamination, the heavier weight keeps the card rigid.
  • Premium positioning. Real estate brokers, law firms, private clinics, luxury retail and hospitality brands often choose the extra heft for senior staff.
  • Tactile finishes. Deep debossing, blind embossing and foil-and-emboss combinations benefit from the thicker board.

Beyond 400 gsm

If 400 gsm still feels too light for the impression you want, duplexed and triplexed cards bond two or three sheets together to create a much thicker card, sometimes with a coloured core visible along the edge. These are a different category of product rather than a slightly heavier version of the same card, and they suit brands where the card itself is part of the identity. Our guide to the best paper for business cards covers those specialty options.

How to decide with confidence

  1. Confirm the stock type first. Coated, uncoated and cotton boards behave differently at the same weight.
  2. Decide on lamination. If yes, 350 gsm is often enough; if no, consider 400 gsm.
  3. List your finishes. Deep embossing or debossing points towards the heavier board.
  4. Think about volume. Cost differences are modest per card, but they add up across a large team.
  5. Handle both weights if you can. The difference is easier to feel than to describe.

Both weights make a good business card; the right one depends on your design, your finishes and how many cards you get through. Frequently asked questions

Is 400 gsm much thicker than 350 gsm?

It is noticeably stiffer rather than dramatically thicker. A 400 gsm board contains about one-seventh more material than a 350 gsm board of the same type, so it bends less and feels more substantial. Remember that stock type matters too: an uncoated 350 gsm card can feel as thick as a coated 400 gsm card, so compare like with like.

Does lamination make 350 gsm feel like 400 gsm?

Lamination adds a thin film to the card, which increases stiffness and gives a more solid feel. A 350 gsm card laminated on both sides can feel close to an unlaminated 400 gsm card, though the surface will feel different. Many businesses choose laminated 350 gsm as a balance of durability, feel and cost, especially for darker designs that need scuff protection.

Which weight is better for embossed business cards?

Embossing and debossing press the design into the board, so a thicker stock gives the impression more depth and definition. A 400 gsm board, or a softer cotton stock, generally holds a cleaner, deeper impression than 350 gsm. For light embossing on a logo or name, 350 gsm can still work well.
