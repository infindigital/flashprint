How Much Does Business Card Printing Cost in Dubai?

The factors that shape the price of a business card order in Dubai, and how to brief your printer so every quote is comparable.

By the Flash Print Solution team11 September 20266 min read

In this article

  1. Why "the same" business cards get very different quotes
  2. Quantity and printing method
  3. Card stock: weight, surface and construction
  4. How the design itself affects cost
  5. Size, shape and artwork readiness
  6. Where the cost comes from at a glance
  7. How to brief for an accurate quote
  8. FAQs

Ask a few printers to quote for "a box of business cards" and the replies can look impossible to compare. The gap rarely comes from margin alone: business card printing cost in Dubai is driven by a handful of specification choices, most of which you control. This guide explains each factor, why it matters in production, and how to brief a printer so every quote is priced on the same basis.

Why "the same" business cards get very different quotes

A business card looks simple, but its quote is built from several separate stages: prepress, printing, finishing, cutting and packing. Each stage has a setup element that costs much the same whether you order a small batch or a large one, plus a running element that grows with every card produced.

When one printer assumes a standard coated board with no finish and another assumes a heavier board with lamination, their figures will never line up. The most useful thing you can do before requesting prices is to fix the specification in writing. Once quantity, stock, sides, finishes and artwork status are agreed, differences between quotes start to mean something.

Quantity and printing method

Quantity has the biggest single effect on the cost of each card. Setup work, such as checking files, preparing the press and programming the cutter, is shared across the whole run, so the unit cost falls as the order grows. The saving is not linear, though. Beyond a certain point each extra card saves very little, and ordering far more than you will use before a job title or phone number changes is false economy.

The printing method works hand in hand with quantity. Digital printing needs minimal setup, which suits smaller runs, staff sets with many different names, and reprints for a single new starter. Offset printing involves making plates and bringing the press up to colour before the run begins, so it tends to pay off on larger volumes, on exact brand colours mixed as spot inks, or when many names can be ganged together on one press sheet. Our comparison of digital vs offset printing explains the trade-offs in more depth.

Number of names and versions

A company ordering cards for twelve people is really placing twelve small jobs, each with its own proof to approve. Collecting names into one batch, using one master template and one approval round, reduces administration and makes better use of each press sheet. If your team grows regularly, a locked template also makes future reorders easier to quote and check.

Card stock: weight, surface and construction

The board you choose moves the price more than many buyers expect. A smooth coated card in the 300 to 400 gsm range is the everyday baseline. Moving to textured, cotton, kraft, colour-core or duplexed board raises the material cost and often adds handling steps.

  • Weight: heavier boards cost more per sheet and need more care through cutting and finishing.
  • Surface: matte, silk and gloss coated boards are widely stocked, while specialty textures such as linen or felt are usually ordered in for the job.
  • Construction: duplexed and triplexed cards are made by bonding two or three sheets together, sometimes with a coloured layer in the middle, which is an extra process in itself.
  • Synthetic cards: PVC and other plastic stocks need compatible inks and a different workflow, so they are quoted separately.

If you are still deciding on a board, our guide to the best paper for business cards compares the common options by feel and finish compatibility.

How the design itself affects cost

Print sides, colour and ink coverage

A single-sided card is simpler to produce than a double-sided one, because the reverse needs another pass or duplex setup and a second round of registration checks. Full-bleed designs, where colour runs off every edge, need accurate trimming, and large dark solids can show scuffing on the face and lighter edges after cutting. Those designs usually call for lamination, which is another step in the job.

Brand colour requirements also matter. If your guidelines specify an exact Pantone colour rather than a CMYK approximation, the quote may be based on offset printing with a mixed ink, which changes the cost structure compared with a standard four-colour run.

Finishes and embellishments

Finishes turn a plain card into a memorable one, and every finish adds a production step. Some also require one-off tooling, which is a fixed cost whether you order a small quantity or a large one.

  • Lamination (matte, gloss or soft-touch) protects the print and adds rigidity in one extra pass.
  • Spot UV applies clear gloss varnish to selected areas and needs its own separate artwork layer.
  • Foil stamping transfers metallic foil with heat and pressure, usually from a metal die made to your artwork.
  • Embossing and debossing press a raised or recessed design into the board using a matched die.
  • Rounded corners, die-cut shapes and painted edges are further finishing steps after printing and trimming.

Combining finishes multiplies the work. A foil logo on soft-touch lamination with spot UV on the reverse involves several separate passes, each with its own setup and inspection. Often a single, well-placed metallic accent carries a design better than three effects competing for attention, as our gold foil business cards show.

Size, shape and artwork readiness

Standard formats such as 90 × 55 mm or 85 × 55 mm fit efficiently on press sheets and standard cutting routines. Square cards, folded cards and custom outlines use sheet space less efficiently and may need a cutting die, so they change the price even when the printed design is identical.

Artwork is the factor buyers overlook most often. A print-ready PDF with 3 mm bleed, embedded or outlined fonts, CMYK colour and separate layers for any foil or spot UV can move straight to proofing. Files that need rebuilding, logos that must be redrawn from a low-resolution image, or a design created from scratch all add work before production. If your files need attention, print-ready file setup can be quoted alongside the printing.

How to brief for an accurate quote

  1. State the quantity per name and the total number of names.
  2. Give the finished size and shape.
  3. Name the stock, or describe the feel you want: crisp and smooth, thick and soft, or textured.
  4. Confirm whether you need printing on one side or both.
  5. List each finish and exactly where it applies, for example foil on the logo only.
  6. Say whether artwork is print-ready, needs checking, or needs designing.
  7. Share your deadline so the printer can tell you which options fit the schedule.

With that brief in hand, every quote you receive can be compared line by line. Timelines depend on quantity, finish and artwork readiness, and we confirm the schedule with your quote before production starts.

The right card is the one that represents your brand properly within the budget you have set, and that balance is far easier to strike once the specification is clear. Frequently asked questions

Why do business card quotes in Dubai vary so much?

Quotes usually differ because each printer is pricing a slightly different specification. Stock weight and coating, finishes, the number of names and whether artwork needs work all change the cost. Sending the same written brief to every printer, covering quantity, size, stock, sides, finishes and file status, is the simplest way to receive quotes you can compare fairly.

Does ordering more business cards reduce the cost per card?

Generally, yes. Setup work such as file checks, press preparation and cutter programming is shared across the whole run, so the cost of each card falls as quantity rises. The saving flattens out at higher volumes, so it makes sense to order what you will realistically hand out before any contact details or job titles change.

Which finishes add the most to a business card order?

Finishes that need custom tooling, such as foil stamping, embossing and die-cut shapes, add a fixed setup element plus an extra production pass. Layering several finishes on one card multiplies those steps. Lamination and rounded corners are simpler additions. If budget matters, one signature finish usually gives a stronger result than several competing effects.

Can I reduce cost by supplying my own artwork?

Supplying a print-ready file removes design and rebuilding time from the job. A print-ready business card file is typically a PDF at finished size plus 3 mm bleed, with fonts embedded or outlined, CMYK colour, and separate layers for any foil or spot UV. We check every file before proofing and flag anything that needs correcting.
