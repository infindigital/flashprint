How Long Does Printing Take in Dubai?

What really sets a print timeline? Artwork, proofs, quantity, materials, finishing, drying and installation access, plus how urgent requests generally work.

By the Flash Print Solution team11 September 20266 min read

In this article

  1. The stages every print job passes through
  2. What really determines the timeline
  3. Timing factors by job type
  4. How express and same-day requests generally work
  5. How to keep your job on schedule
  6. Planning backwards from a deadline
  7. The practical takeaway
  8. FAQs

The most common question in any print shop is how long a job will take, and the honest answer is that it depends on a handful of predictable factors. Once you know what they are, you can plan printing turnaround in Dubai around your launch, event or opening date instead of hoping it works out. This guide walks through what drives timelines and how urgent requests are generally handled across the industry.

One point before we start: at Flash Print Solution we confirm the schedule for each job with the quote, based on the specification, quantity and artwork you provide. The factors below explain why that schedule varies from job to job.

The stages every print job passes through

Whatever the product, most jobs follow the same sequence. Each stage has its own duration, and a delay at one pushes back everything after it.

  1. Brief and quote: specification, quantity, finish and delivery or installation details are agreed.
  2. Artwork check: files are checked for size, bleed, resolution, colour and fonts.
  3. Proof: a digital or physical proof is prepared for your review.
  4. Approval: you sign off the proof, and the job enters the production schedule.
  5. Printing: on the method suited to the product and quantity.
  6. Drying or curing: inks and coatings settle before they can be handled further.
  7. Finishing: cutting, laminating, folding, binding, foiling, mounting or fabrication.
  8. Quality check and packing.
  9. Delivery or installation.

Our how it works page shows how this sequence runs for a typical order.

What really determines the timeline

Artwork readiness

This is the biggest variable, and the one most within your control. Print-ready files move straight to proofing. Files that need resizing, missing fonts, low-resolution images, added bleed or Arabic text corrections have to go back and forth first. If design work is still in progress, the print timeline has not really started. Our guide to print-ready file requirements lists what printers look for.

Proof approval

Every round of changes means a new proof and a new wait. Jobs slow down most when feedback arrives from several people at different times. Collecting all comments into one consolidated round keeps things moving.

Quantity and print method

Short runs are usually printed digitally, which needs little set-up. Longer runs of brochures, catalogues or stationery may go to offset, which involves plates and make-ready but then runs efficiently at volume. The method affects when a job can be scheduled on press, and the trade-offs are explained in digital vs offset printing.

Stock and material availability

Standard papers, vinyls and banner materials are generally easier to source. Speciality boards, textured papers, particular metallic films or specific acrylic colours may need to be ordered in, which adds a step before production can begin.

Finishing complexity

A trimmed flyer is simple. Hot foil needs a foil block made from your artwork, custom shapes need a cutting die, embossing needs a matched die pair, and bound documents need folding, collating and binding. Each of these adds preparation and machine time.

Drying and curing time

Some steps cannot be compressed. Offset sheets need to dry before they are cut or laminated. Large-format prints made with solvent-based inks need time for the solvents to escape before lamination, or the laminate can lift or bubble later. Adhesives on mounted signs need time to bond. Skipping these steps saves time on paper but creates problems in use.

Fabrication and installation scheduling

Signage, wall graphics and vehicle branding depend on more than the print. Fabricating frames, lettering or light boxes has its own sequence, and installation needs site access, working-at-height equipment where relevant, and permission from landlords or mall management. Many malls restrict fitting work to specific hours, and building management may need to approve the design before installation.

How express and same-day requests generally work

Across the printing industry, urgent turnaround is less a separate product than a set of conditions. Express or same-day printing becomes realistic when artwork is final and correct, the stock is already on the shelf, the finishing is simple and the quantity is modest. Printers then try to fit the job into a production schedule that already has other commitments, so availability changes from day to day.

Urgency usually involves trade-offs. You may be offered a standard stock instead of a speciality paper, a digital run instead of offset, or a simpler finish in place of foil or a custom shape. Some printers also price urgent slots differently, so it is worth asking. What cannot be hurried are the physical steps: drying, curing, adhesive bonding and third-party approvals take the time they take.

If your deadline is tight, say so at the start and share the date alongside your specification. We do not commit to a timeline until we have seen the job details; we confirm what is achievable in your quote, and suggest alternatives where a particular finish or material would not fit the date.

How to keep your job on schedule

  • Send final, print-ready files, or ask for artwork help early so it is built into the plan.
  • Name one person to approve proofs and gather all feedback into a single round.
  • Confirm quantity, size and finish before proofing, not after.
  • Choose standard materials where a speciality stock is not essential.
  • Share the delivery address, site contact and access rules for installation jobs.
  • Ask landlords or mall management about approval requirements early.
  • Build in a buffer before events so a small correction does not put the date at risk.

Planning backwards from a deadline

The most reliable method is to start with the date you need the item in use and work backwards. For an exhibition, the chain runs: stand opening, installation slot, delivery to the venue, finishing, printing, proof approval, artwork completion and, before all of that, the design brief. Put a realistic allowance against each step, ask your printer to confirm the production portion, and you will soon see whether the design stage needs to start now. For event materials such as roll-up banners and backdrops, this approach removes most last-minute pressure.

The practical takeaway

Printing timelines are shaped by artwork, approvals, quantity, materials, finishing, drying and installation access. Control the first two and the rest becomes much easier to plan. When you are ready, send your brief and target date and we will confirm the schedule with your quote before production starts.

Frequently asked questions

Can you give me a standard turnaround time?

Timelines depend on quantity, material, finishing and how ready the artwork is, so we confirm the schedule with each quote rather than quoting a fixed figure. If you share your specification and the date you need the items, we can tell you what is achievable and suggest adjustments, such as a different finish or material, if the date is tight.

Why does lamination or mounting add time to large-format prints?

Many large-format inks need time to dry and release solvents before a laminate is applied. If the film goes on too soon, trapped solvents can cause bubbling, lifting or a cloudy finish later. Mounting onto boards and bonding adhesives also need time to set properly. These steps protect the final quality and are difficult to shorten without risk.

What is the easiest way to avoid delays?

Send final, print-ready artwork and appoint one person to approve the proof. Most delays come from incomplete files and multiple rounds of changes rather than from the printing itself. For signage and installation jobs, sharing site access details and any landlord or mall requirements early also removes a common cause of rescheduling.
