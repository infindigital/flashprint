Spot UV Printing: What It Is and When to Use It

How spot UV works, why it looks best on matte laminated stock, where it suits cards, folders and packaging, and how to prepare the artwork.

By the Flash Print Solution team14 September 20266 min read

In this article

  2. How spot UV is produced
  3. Why it looks best over matte lamination
  4. Where spot UV works well
  5. Spot UV, foil and embossing compared
  6. Setting up artwork for spot UV
  7. Limitations to plan around
  8. FAQs

Tilt a business card under the light and watch a logo shine against a flat, velvety background: that contrast is spot UV. It is a popular finish for cards, folders, brochure covers and packaging because it adds depth and texture without adding a single extra colour. This article explains how spot UV printing works, where it looks its best, and how to prepare artwork so the result is crisp.

What spot UV actually is

Spot UV is a clear, glossy varnish applied only to selected areas of a printed piece, the "spots". The varnish is cured instantly by ultraviolet light, which hardens it into a shiny, durable layer. Everything outside those areas keeps its original finish.

The name describes the process rather than the look. "Spot" means it is applied to chosen areas, as opposed to a flood coating that covers the whole sheet, and "UV" refers to the curing method. The result can be flat, sitting almost level with the surface, or raised, where a thicker layer creates a tactile relief you can feel with a fingertip.

How spot UV is produced

There are two main production routes. Traditional spot UV is applied through a screen, much like screen printing, with a separate screen made for each design. Digital spot UV uses an inkjet coating unit that lays varnish directly from the file, with no screen needed, and it can build up thicker layers for a raised effect.

Either way, the varnish is applied after printing and, in most cases, after lamination. That order matters. Spot UV needs a smooth, sealed surface to sit on. Applied onto uncoated paper, the varnish soaks in and loses its shine, so the finish is almost always specified over matte or soft-touch lamination.

Variations worth knowing

  • Raised or 3D spot UV: a thicker layer you can feel, often used on logos and names.
  • Textured UV: varnishes with a sand, leather or pattern effect for tactile contrast.
  • Glitter UV: varnish containing fine glitter, used on invitations and festive packaging.

Why it looks best over matte lamination

Spot UV works through contrast. A gloss highlight on a gloss background is almost invisible, while the same highlight on a matte or soft-touch surface stands out sharply, both visually and to the touch. That is why the classic combination is a matte laminated card with a spot UV logo. Our guide to matte vs glossy printing explains the base finishes in more detail.

The effect is strongest on dark, flat colours. A black-on-black design, where a black logo is varnished over a black matte background, is subtle in a way many brands like: the detail appears only when the light catches it.

Where spot UV works well

  • Business cards: a varnished logo, name or pattern turns a simple card into a tactile one. See our business card printing options.
  • Presentation folders: a spot UV logo on a matte laminated cover gives presentation folders a polished, corporate look.
  • Brochure and catalogue covers: highlighting the title or a key image draws the eye on a table or display.
  • Packaging: brand marks and product names on custom boxes gain depth on the shelf.
  • Invitations: patterns, monograms and names add a sense of occasion.

These finishes can also be combined. A foil logo with a spot UV pattern around it is a common premium pairing, as explained in our foil printing guide.

Setting up artwork for spot UV

The varnish needs its own instructions, separate from the printed colours. Following a few rules avoids most problems:

  1. Create a separate layer or file for the spot UV areas, exactly the same size and position as the print artwork.
  2. Fill UV areas with 100% black in vector form. Black means varnish, white means no varnish; no greys, gradients or transparency.
  3. Keep detail generous. As a rule of thumb, avoid lines thinner than about 0.25 mm and text smaller than about 8 pt, as fine detail can fill in or break up.
  4. Allow for registration tolerance. The varnish may sit fractionally off the printed element, so avoid designs where UV must align perfectly with very thin printed outlines.
  5. Keep UV away from folds and trims. Varnish can crack across a fold, so leave a clear margin around score lines and the cut edge.
  6. Name layers clearly, for example "SPOT UV", so nothing is misread in prepress.

Limitations to plan around

Spot UV is not suited to uncoated or heavily textured stocks, and it does not add colour, so it relies on a good contrast with the base. Large raised areas can feel heavy, and any extra finish adds a production step to the job. Timelines depend on quantity, finish and artwork readiness, and we confirm the schedule with your quote.

Used with restraint, spot UV is one of the most effective ways to give print a premium, tactile quality. Send your artwork or idea through our contact page and we will check the UV layer, recommend a base finish and confirm the options in your quote.

Frequently asked questions

Can spot UV be applied to any paper?

Spot UV needs a smooth, sealed surface to stay glossy. It is usually applied over matte or soft-touch lamination, or on smooth coated stock. On uncoated or heavily textured paper the varnish soaks in or sits unevenly and loses the contrast that makes it effective. We confirm whether your chosen stock suits spot UV when we prepare your quote.

What is the difference between flat and raised spot UV?

Flat spot UV is a thin layer of gloss varnish that sits almost level with the surface, giving a visual contrast of shine against matte. Raised spot UV is built up in a thicker layer, so you can feel the design with your fingertip as well as see it. Raised UV suits logos and names; very fine detail works better flat.

How do I supply artwork for spot UV?

Supply your print artwork as normal, plus a separate layer or file for the varnish, exactly the same size and position. Fill every area to be varnished with 100% black as vector shapes, with no gradients or tints. Keep lines and text reasonably bold, and leave a margin around folds and trim edges. We check the UV layer during the artwork review.
