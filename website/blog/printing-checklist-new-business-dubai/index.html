The Printing Checklist for a New Business in Dubai

Brand files, stationery, signage, launch materials and sector-specific extras, organised into a phased plan so nothing is left until the week you open.

By the Flash Print Solution team11 September 20266 min read

In this article

  1. Before you print anything: get the foundations right
  2. Identity and business stationery
  3. Signage for your premises
  4. Launch and marketing materials
  5. Extras by sector
  6. A phased plan

  8. FAQs

Setting up a company in Dubai involves licences, leases and fit-out, and printed items are often left until the week before opening. A clear printing checklist for a new business keeps brand materials, signage and stationery moving alongside the practical set-up rather than trailing behind it. Use the lists below as a starting point and strike out anything that does not apply to your sector.

Before you print anything: get the foundations right

Every item on this checklist draws on the same few assets. Sorting them out first prevents inconsistent colours, stretched logos and reprints.

  • Logo files: a vector master (AI, EPS, SVG or PDF), plus versions for light and dark backgrounds.
  • Colour references: CMYK values for print, and Pantone references if close colour matching matters.
  • Fonts: licensed files for your brand typefaces, including an Arabic font if you use bilingual materials.
  • Confirmed details: trade name exactly as licensed, address, phone, email, website and social handles.

If the brand is not yet finalised, logo design is the place to start, because every other item depends on it.

Identity and business stationery

These are the items clients, suppliers and officials see first.

  • Business cards for founders and client-facing staff.
  • Letterheads for quotations, contracts and formal letters.
  • Envelopes in the sizes you actually post or hand-deliver.
  • Presentation folders for proposals and client packs.
  • Invoice and receipt books, or NCR books for delivery notes and job cards that need copies.
  • A company profile, printed as a booklet or short brochure, for tenders and meetings.

A few specification points are worth settling early. Business cards in the UAE are typically 90 × 55 mm, and card weights around 350 to 400 gsm feel substantial without being hard to carry. Letterheads are usually A4 on uncoated paper of around 100 to 120 gsm; if you plan to run them through an office printer, say so, because some coatings and finishes do not suit laser printers. Ordering cards, letterheads and envelopes together makes it easier to keep paper colour and brand colours matched across the set.

If you are registered for VAT, check your invoice layout with your accountant before printing invoice books, since tax invoices must carry specific details such as your tax registration number.

Signage for your premises

Signage involves measurements, approvals and installation, so it needs the longest lead-in of anything on this list. Start as soon as the premises are confirmed.

  • Exterior or shopfront sign: produced to the specification your landlord, mall or relevant authority provides, so ask for their signage guidelines early. See shop signage for common constructions.
  • Office name board or reception sign: the first thing visitors see inside.
  • Glass manifestation and privacy film: frosted film or printed graphics on glass partitions and doors.
  • Wayfinding and room signs: meeting rooms, washrooms and staff-only areas.
  • Safety signs: fire exits, assembly points and equipment signs, as your fit-out consultant or authority requires.
  • Opening hours and payment stickers for doors and counters.

For the exterior sign, think about how it will be seen: from the road, from a mall corridor or from across a car park. That affects letter height, whether illumination is worth considering and which construction suits the location. Exterior signs in Dubai commonly show the trade name in both Arabic and English, so confirm the wording and layout requirements with your landlord or the relevant authority before design is finalised.

Launch and marketing materials

  • Flyers or leaflets for local distribution and walk-in customers.
  • Roll-up banners for events, lobbies and pop-up stands.
  • Posters and window graphics announcing the opening.
  • Branded stickers for packaging, bags and deliveries.
  • Promotional items such as pens, mugs or notebooks for early clients and partners.

Keep launch materials tied to a specific plan. Decide where flyers will be distributed and how many locations you can realistically cover, and give each piece a clear call to action, such as an opening offer or a QR code linking to your booking page. A roll-up banner designed without dates or time-limited offers can be reused at later events long after the launch.

Extras by sector

Restaurants and cafés

Menus, tent cards, takeaway menus, cup and box stickers, delivery bag labels, staff uniforms and a menu board near the entrance. Menus are the item most likely to change in the first weeks, as dishes and prices are adjusted, so plan a format that is easy to reprint.

Retail

Price tags and labels, shopping bags, window displays, shelf talkers and counter displays. Carrier bags and product labels usually need the longest planning in this group, because sizes, handles and materials have to be agreed before artwork is finished.

Clinics and healthcare

Patient forms, prescription pads, appointment cards and clear directional signage. Forms often go through several internal revisions, so confirm the final wording with the medical team before committing to a print run.

Offices and consultancies

Company profiles, presentation folders, office branding and meeting-room signage. A company profile is often the first document a prospective client reads, so allow time for copywriting and photography as well as print.

Avoiding wasted print, and next steps

  • Do not print anything with contact details until the phone number, address and licensed name are final.
  • Order modest first runs of items likely to change, such as menus and price lists, then reorder once they settle.
  • Keep one set of master files and share them with every supplier, so colours and logos stay consistent.
  • Request a proof for every item, and have someone who did not design it read it.
  • Where colour matters, approve a physical sample before a large run.

Working through this checklist with one supplier makes it easier to keep colours, finishes and logos consistent from the business card to the shopfront. Frequently asked questions

What should a new business print first?

Start with the brand files, then signage, because signage needs measurements, landlord or mall approvals and installation. Stationery such as business cards and letterheads can follow once your trade name, address and phone numbers are confirmed. Launch materials like flyers and banners come last, timed to your opening. Printing contact details too early is one of the most common causes of reprints.

Do I need approval for my shop sign in Dubai?

Many landlords, malls and communities have their own signage guidelines, and some signs need approval from the relevant authority before installation. Requirements vary by location, so ask your landlord or mall management for their guidelines as soon as the lease is confirmed. The sign can then be designed and produced to the specification they provide.

How many business cards should a new company order?

A moderate first order per person is usually sensible, because job titles, phone numbers and even office addresses often change in the first months of trading. Once details are stable, larger reorders make more sense. Digital printing suits smaller runs and individual names, while offset becomes relevant for larger, repeated orders of the same design.
