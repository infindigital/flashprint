Choosing a Printing Company in Dubai: A Buyer's Checklist

A neutral buyer's checklist for choosing a printer in Dubai: briefs, file checks, proofing, materials advice, samples, installation capability, communication and reviews.

By the Flash Print Solution team11 September 20266 min read

In this article

  2. File checks and proofing
  3. Materials advice and samples
  4. Capability and installation
  5. Communication, reviews and warning signs
  6. Questions to put to every printer

  8. FAQs

Most print buyers only discover how good a printer is when something goes wrong: a colour shift, a misspelt name, a sign that arrives without fixings. Knowing how to choose a printing company in Dubai before placing the first order saves that discovery for someone else. This neutral checklist covers the points that separate a dependable print partner from a simple quote comparison.

Start with a clear brief

Quotes can only be compared when they describe the same job. Before contacting printers, write down the specification so every supplier responds to the same thing.

  • Item, finished size and quantity.
  • Material or paper weight if you know it, or how the item will be used if you do not.
  • Finishes such as lamination, foil, spot UV or custom cutting.
  • Whether artwork is ready or design help is needed.
  • Delivery location, and whether installation is required.
  • The date you need the items in use.

When two quotes differ significantly, the specification is usually the reason. A clear brief shows you whether you are comparing like with like.

File checks and proofing

A careful printer checks your artwork before it reaches production and tells you about problems such as low resolution, missing bleed, RGB colour or fonts that have not embedded. Ask how files are checked and what happens when something is wrong.

Ask about proofs too. A digital PDF proof confirms layout and text. A physical proof or printed sample shows colour and finish, which matters for brand colours, packaging and large runs. Find out how approval works, and what the process is if a delivered job does not match the approved proof. Our article on print-ready file requirements explains what a thorough file check covers.

Materials advice and samples

Advice that starts with questions

A good printer asks questions before recommending a material: where the sign will be installed, whether the menu will be wiped down, whether the sticker is going on a chilled bottle. The answers change the right choice, and a supplier who simply prints whatever is ordered may leave you with a product that fails in use.

Listen for honest trade-offs. A printer who explains why unlaminated card will mark in a busy restaurant, or why a paper sticker will not survive outdoors, is protecting your budget as well as their own reputation.

Samples and portfolio evidence

  • Printed samples: ask to handle examples of similar work, such as business cards with the finish you want, or stickers on the proposed material.
  • Paper and material swatches: handling stocks in person tells you more than a paper weight on a quote.
  • Photos of completed work: for signage and installations, look for photos of finished jobs on site, not only design mock-ups.
  • Relevant experience: a portfolio in your product type or sector shows the printer understands its demands.

Capability and installation

Printing a job and delivering a finished result are not always the same thing. Ask which parts of the work are handled in-house and which are arranged with partners. Neither is automatically a problem, but it affects how quality and timing are controlled.

For signage, wall graphics, glass film and vehicle branding, installation is part of the product. Ask whether the printer has its own installation team, how site surveys and measurements are handled, and whether they can work at height or outside mall trading hours when required. It helps to understand what professional sign board installation involves before you compare offers.

Communication, reviews and warning signs

What good communication looks like

  • A written quote that lists size, quantity, material, finish and any delivery or installation.
  • A clear point of contact who answers questions without long gaps.
  • Timelines confirmed against your specification rather than a vague promise.
  • A defined process for changes, extra proofs and reprints.

Ask the printer to walk you through their process from brief to delivery. A supplier with a defined workflow can explain each stage clearly and tell you where your input is needed.

Reading reviews and asking for references

Online reviews are useful when you read them for patterns rather than star ratings alone. Look for comments about how problems were resolved, whether deadlines were communicated honestly and whether colour and finish matched expectations. For larger projects such as signage or fit-out graphics, ask for a reference you can speak to directly.

Warning signs to watch for

  • A quote with a total but no specification, so you cannot tell what is included.
  • A delivery date promised before anyone has seen your files or confirmed the material.
  • No proof offered, or pressure to approve a proof without time to check it.
  • Reluctance to show physical samples or photos of completed installations.
  • Vague answers about who installs signage and how it will be fixed.

None of these automatically rules a supplier out, but each deserves a direct question before you commit.

Making the choice

The right printing company asks good questions, checks your files, proofs properly, explains material choices and communicates clearly, with evidence of similar work to back it up. Use this checklist with every supplier you speak to, including us. Our how it works page sets out the process we follow from brief to delivery, and you can send a brief for a quote whenever you are ready to compare.

Frequently asked questions

Should I always choose the lowest quote?

Not necessarily. Quotes often differ because of material, finish, file checking, proofing or installation details rather than the printing alone. Compare the full specification line by line and ask what is included. A lower quote that leaves out proofing, uses a thinner stock or excludes installation fixings can end up costing more if the job has to be redone.

Is a physical proof necessary?

For simple jobs such as a reprint of existing business cards, a digital proof is usually enough to confirm text and layout. For brand colours, packaging, special finishes or large runs, a physical proof or printed sample shows how the colour and finish will look on the actual material. Ask whether one is available when you request a quote.

What should I ask about installation?

Ask who carries out the installation, whether a site survey or measurement visit is included, how the item will be fixed to your wall, glass or facade, and whether the team can work at height or within mall access hours. For signage, also ask how landlord or mall guidelines are handled, since many sites have specific requirements.
