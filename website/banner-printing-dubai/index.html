About banner printing

Banner printing in Dubai is rarely a one-size job. The same promotion might need a 3 × 1 m flex banner over a shop entrance, a mesh banner along a site fence and a fabric banner behind a conference stage. This page is the starting point for all of them: it explains the main banner types, how each is finished and which suits which location. Where a format deserves more detail, such as PVC flex, roll-up stands or X-banners, we link to its own page.

A few practical questions shape every banner: where it will hang and for how long, how far away people will read it, and what it will be fixed to. Once those are clear, the material, ink, reinforcement and fixing usually follow.

For outdoor banners in the UAE we generally recommend PVC flex or mesh printed with solvent or UV-curable inks, which resist fading better than water-based inks. Indoors, polyester fabric or smooth PVC gives a finer finish with less glare under spotlights. We confirm the material, size and finishing in your quote before anything is printed.

Materials

Frontlit PVC flex

The standard outdoor banner material, typically 440 to 510 gsm. It is strong, copes with heat and dust, and takes hems and eyelets well.

PVC mesh

An open-weave banner, commonly 270 to 370 gsm, that lets wind pass through. The sensible choice for fences, scaffolding and exposed rooftop positions.

Blockout flex

Flex with a light-blocking core, so a double-sided banner shows no ghosting of the reverse image. Used for hanging and lamp-post banners.

Polyester fabric

A soft, matt textile for stages, lobbies and events. It folds for transport and photographs without the glare of PVC.

Finishing options

Hem and eyelet

Edges folded and heat-welded, with eyelets typically every 50 cm for cable ties or rope. The everyday finish for wall and fence banners.

Pole pockets

Sleeves at the top and bottom, or sides, for poles, battens or lamp-post brackets. The banner hangs neatly with no visible ties.

Welded panels

Wide banners printed in sections and heat-welded into one piece, with seams planned away from faces and headlines.

Wind slits

Small cuts in solid flex that relieve some wind pressure. On very exposed sites, mesh is usually the better answer.

Artwork checklist

  • Supply artwork at full size or to scale, with the final dimensions written on the file
  • Keep text and logos at least 50 mm inside every edge to clear hems and eyelets
  • Aim for 100 to 150 ppi at full size for close viewing; 30 to 50 ppi is plenty for banners read from across a road
  • Keep logos and text as vector outlines and convert fonts to curves
  • Give Pantone references for brand colours so we can match them as closely as the media allows

Banner printing FAQs

Is a banner the same as a flex banner?

Not always. Banner is the umbrella term for any printed, flexible sign that hangs or is stretched in place. Flex refers to the PVC material that most outdoor banners use. Fabric, mesh and vinyl banners are alternatives with a different weight, finish and behaviour in wind. Our flex banner page covers PVC flex grades in more depth.

How do I work out the right banner size?

Start with the space and the viewing distance. Measure the wall, fence or frame, including where fixings can go, then think about how far away most people will be. Text read from across a car park needs large, bold lettering, so a short headline works better than a paragraph. Send photos of the location and we will suggest proportions.

Can a banner be printed on both sides?

Yes. Double-sided banners are printed on blockout flex, which has a dark core that stops the reverse image showing through when light hits it. They are common for banners hung across walkways, from mall ceilings or on lamp posts, where people approach from both directions. Tell us if each side carries different artwork.

Which banner suits an indoor event?

For stages, ballrooms and conference rooms, polyester fabric is often preferred because it is matt, photographs without glare and folds for transport. Smooth PVC works well where the banner will be rolled and reused. For free-standing signs at registration or entrances, a roll-up or X-banner is usually more practical than a hung banner.
