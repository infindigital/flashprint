Interior graphics in Dubai sit closer to interior design than to signage. A printed wallcovering in a hotel corridor, a run of photographic panels in a developer's sales gallery, canvas artwork in serviced apartments or a seasonal scheme on a mall shopfront are all chosen as finishes, and they have to hold their own beside stone, timber and carefully planned lighting.

We produce four product families in this category: custom wallpaper on non-woven and commercial vinyl wallcoverings; interior panels on rigid boards such as foam PVC, aluminium composite and acrylic; canvas prints on stretched timber frames; and window display graphics for retail frontages. Interior designers and fit-out contractors can send specifications and drawings, while business owners can simply start with a photo of the space.

Matching the product to the space

The choice usually comes down to three questions: how permanent the graphic should be, how much of the surface it covers, and how that surface will be used and cleaned. A hotel refurbishment might combine printed wallpaper in guest rooms with canvas above the beds, while a sales centre might pair photographic panels inside with window graphics facing the car park.

  • Wallpaper printing: full-room or feature-wall coverage with patterns or imagery, hung with paste like any wallcovering
  • Interior panels: rigid printed boards fixed to walls, columns or partitions, removable and relocatable
  • Canvas printing: framed artwork and photography for rooms, suites, corridors and meeting spaces
  • Window displays: shopfront graphics and suspended displays that change with each campaign

Specifications interior designers ask for

Commercial interiors often arrive with a specification covering wallcovering weight and backing, fire classification, colour references, washability and substrate thickness. Share it at the start and we source materials to it, confirming stock and options in your quote. Where no specification exists, we propose materials based on footfall, cleaning routines and how long the graphic should stay in place.

Colour consistency matters more here than in most print work, because interior graphics sit next to painted walls, upholstery and brand elements. For colour-critical schemes we print strike-offs of wallpaper and panels for sign-off, and we produce all the pieces of one scheme together so batches match. We also ask about lighting, because warm LED, cool daylight lamps and strong window light each shift how printed colour reads, so strike-offs are best judged in the room itself.

Installation and site coordination

Wallpaper and large panels are installed by our team at your premises in Dubai, with walls checked for moisture, level and finish before work begins. On fit-out projects we coordinate with the main contractor so graphics go in after painting and flooring but before furniture arrives, which avoids damage and rework.

Production time varies with quantity, materials and artwork readiness, so the schedule is confirmed alongside your quote. Retail window installations are usually booked outside trading hours, and taking down the previous campaign can be handled in the same visit.

Aftercare is simple for most of these products. Laminated panels and scrubbable wallcoverings need only a soft cloth and mild detergent, while canvas should simply be dusted. Ordering a spare wallpaper drop or panel with the main run makes later repairs much easier to match.

Tell us where it will be used, how long it needs to last and your quantity. We'll recommend the right architectural & interior graphics option.

FAQs

Architectural & Interior Graphics FAQs

What is the difference between wallpaper printing and a wall mural?

Printed wallpaper is a wallcovering, usually a repeating pattern run across whole rooms or corridors and hung in strips with paste. A wall mural is a single large image scaled to one specific wall. The media can be similar, but the design approach differs: wallpaper is planned around repeats and roll widths, a mural around one composition.

When are interior panels a better choice than graphics applied straight to the wall?

Panels make sense when the wall should not be touched, when the graphic needs to move with you, or when the surface is too rough for vinyl. They also add a few millimetres of depth and a crisp edge. Direct wall graphics sit flatter and suit smooth painted walls where a slim, painted-on look is the goal.

Can you work from an interior designer's specification?

Yes. Send the specification, drawings and finish schedule, and we source materials to match, flagging anything that differs in the quote. We can supply samples or strike-offs for sign-off and coordinate installation with the contractor on site, including the order of works around painting and joinery.

Do malls have rules for window displays?

Most do. Mall retail guidelines usually cover how much glass can be covered, what may be suspended behind the window and when installation can take place. Share your mall's guidelines and we design and produce within them, and prepare mock-ups you can submit to the mall team with your request.
