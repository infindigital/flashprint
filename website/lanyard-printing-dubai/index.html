About lanyard printing

Lanyard printing in Dubai runs on two calendars. Conferences and exhibitions order in bursts for a specific event with the show branding on the strap, while offices, hospitals, schools and construction sites reorder the same staff lanyard year after year. Both need the same three decisions: width, print method and fittings.

Width sets what you can print. A 10 mm strap carries a short name in one colour, 15 mm is the usual event width, and 20 or 25 mm gives room for a logo with a strapline or a repeating pattern. Dye sublimation drives the ink into the polyester so the print sits flush with the weave and appears on both faces, which is what full-colour artwork and gradients need. Screen printing puts a flat spot colour on one or both sides and is economical for a simple logo at volume. Woven lanyards have the text stitched into the ribbon, giving a textured finish with no ink at all.

Fittings decide whether the lanyard actually works with your building. A swivel snap hook suits a punched card, a bulldog clip suits a plastic holder, and a retractable badge reel lets someone tap an access reader without lifting the strap over their head. A safety breakaway at the back of the neck releases under pull and is standard for site, workshop and clinical environments.

Materials

Flat polyester

The standard strap. Smooth, holds a printed logo well and is the base for dye-sublimated and screen-printed lanyards.

Tubular polyester

Woven as a tube for a rounder, softer feel. Takes screen printing but not fine detail, so it suits simple logos.

Satin polyester

Smoother surface with a slight sheen. Prints sharper than flat polyester and is often chosen for conferences and hospitality.

RPET webbing

Strap woven from recycled bottle fibre, printed the same way as standard polyester, for events with a sustainability policy.

Finishing options

Dye sublimation

Ink converted to gas and driven into the fibre, giving full-colour artwork on both faces with no raised ink layer.

Screen printing

Flat spot colour pressed onto one or both sides, one screen per colour. The economical option for a simple logo.

Woven text

Lettering stitched into the ribbon rather than printed. Durable and textured, best for short text and simple marks.

Heat transfer

Printed film pressed onto the strap, used for short runs and for artwork with photographic detail.

Artwork checklist

  • Send a vector logo (AI, EPS, SVG or PDF) set to the strap width you have chosen
  • Allow a 3 mm clear margin along both long edges so the print does not run off the weave
  • Give Pantone references for screen printing; polyester shifts colour slightly from a paper proof
  • Keep text above about 4 mm tall on a 15 mm strap so it stays readable at arm's length
  • Tell us the card type, reader and whether a breakaway is required before fittings are confirmed

Lanyard printing FAQs

Which lanyard width should we choose?

For most events, 15 mm or 20 mm works. A 15 mm strap carries a logo and short text comfortably, and 20 mm gives room for a logo with a strapline or a repeating pattern. Choose 10 mm only for a single short word in one colour. Staff lanyards worn daily are usually 20 mm because the wider strap sits better under a badge.

What is the difference between sublimation and screen printing?

Sublimation drives the ink into the polyester fibres, so the artwork appears on both faces with no raised layer and can include gradients and photographs. Screen printing sits a flat spot colour on the surface, one screen per colour, which is more economical for a simple logo at volume but cannot reproduce fine multi-colour detail.

Do we need a safety breakaway?

On sites, in workshops, in clinical areas and anywhere with machinery, yes. A breakaway is a plastic connector at the back of the neck that separates under pull instead of holding, which prevents the strap catching. Many organisations now fit them as standard for all staff lanyards. They add little cost and can be specified on any width.

Can you supply badge reels and card holders too?

Yes. Retractable badge reels let a card be pulled out to a reader and spring back, which suits tap-to-enter systems. Rigid and soft card holders protect a printed card from cracking. Both can be fitted to the lanyard before delivery, and we can print the ID cards at the same time so the pass arrives complete.
