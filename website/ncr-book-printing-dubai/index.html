About NCR book printing

NCR book printing in Dubai supports the paperwork that keeps sites, warehouses, fleets and service teams running. NCR, or no carbon required, paper copies handwriting through a chemical coating, so one entry produces two, three or four identical copies that go to different people.

Each set is built from three paper types. The top sheet is coated on the back (CB), middle sheets are coated front and back (CFB), and the bottom sheet is coated on the front (CF). Getting the sequence right is what makes the last copy readable, so every set is collated in order and transfer is checked on a sample before finishing.

Unlike invoice books, which follow a fixed sales layout, these forms are designed around your process. A maintenance job card might need time in and out, parts used and a customer signature; a work permit needs hazards, controls and sign-off boxes. We help lay out those fields so they are easy to complete on a clipboard, in a van or at a gatehouse.

Materials

CB (coated back)

The top sheet, coated on the reverse only. It carries the original writing and passes pressure down to the next sheet.

CFB (coated front and back)

Middle sheets that receive an image from above and pass it on below, used in 3 and 4-part sets.

CF (coated front)

The final copy, which receives the image and is usually the one kept in the book or file.

Tinted parts

A different tint for each part shows at a glance which copy belongs to the customer, the office or the site.

Finishing options

Glued pads

Sets are glued along one edge so each complete set lifts off cleanly as one unit.

Bound books

Sets bound into books with a card cover and perforations, so copies tear out while the file copy stays behind.

Desensitising

A coating on selected areas stops information transferring to specific copies, for example keeping costs off the customer copy.

Punching and numbering

Two-hole punching for lever-arch files and sequential numbering for traceability.

Artwork checklist

  • Design in black or one solid colour; heavy solid areas can weaken image transfer
  • Leave boxes at least 7–8 mm high for handwriting and ticks
  • Label each part clearly at the foot, for example 'White: Customer, Yellow: Office'
  • Allow a 12–15 mm margin on the bound edge for glue, binding and punching
  • Mark any areas that should not copy to certain parts, so they can be desensitised
  • Send a filled-in example so we can size fields to real entries

NCR book printing FAQs

What does NCR stand for?

NCR stands for no carbon required. The back of each sheet carries micro-capsules of dye and the front of the sheet below carries a reactive coating, so the pressure of a pen or stamp produces a copy without carbon inserts. It is cleaner than carbon paper, does not smudge on hands and keeps every copy aligned in the set.

How many parts can an NCR set have?

Two, three and four-part sets are common. Beyond four parts, the bottom copy becomes faint because pen pressure spreads through more sheets, so we generally suggest staying within four. If more people need a record, consider scanning the office copy or splitting the form into two separate documents.

Are NCR forms the same as invoice books?

Both use the same carbonless paper, but the purpose differs. Invoice and receipt books follow a standard sales layout with prices and VAT. NCR forms are built for any other multi-copy document, such as job cards, delivery notes and permits, and can be supplied as pads or loose sets as well as books. Many companies order both with matching headers.

Can we have different information on each copy?

Yes. Each part can carry its own footer or title, and selected areas can be desensitised so they do not transfer to certain copies. These details are agreed at the layout stage and shown on the proof, so everyone can see which information appears on which part before the NCR book printing goes ahead.

Should we choose books, pads or loose sets?

Books keep a permanent file copy and suit counters and vehicles. Pads are glued along one edge so complete sets lift off together, which works well on clipboards. Loose collated sets suit forms that are handed out, posted or filed individually. We can recommend a format once we know where the forms are completed and stored.
