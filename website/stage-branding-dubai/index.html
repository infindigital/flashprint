About stage branding

Stage branding in Dubai sits in a busy environment: lighting rigs, LED walls, cameras, speakers and a production crew working to a run-of-show. The printed elements have to fit precisely into that set without reflecting light, blocking camera angles or getting in the way of cables and access. Almost every photo from the event will include the stage, so this is where branding does the most work.

The stage fascia, or skirt, runs along the front edge of the stage deck, usually somewhere between 400 and 1,000 mm high, and is often printed fabric or foamex. Lecterns are wrapped in printed vinyl or fitted with a changeable front panel. Around LED screens, fabric or foamex surrounds hide the frame and extend the branding, while cut-out foamex letters or 3D logos stand on the deck or hang from truss.

Everything is produced to the stage plan supplied by the event production or AV company, since they control dimensions, rigging and load-in. We supply graphics labelled for the load-in window, which at hotels is often overnight, and can install on site where the venue and production team allow.

Stage branding FAQs

What does stage branding usually include?

A typical conference stage has a printed fascia along the front edge, a branded lectern, graphics around or beside the LED screen and sometimes a printed backdrop. Launches and galas add set pieces such as cut-out logos, step risers and floor graphics. We can suggest a package from the stage plan and the budget you have in mind.

How does stage branding differ from an event backdrop?

An event backdrop is a standalone photo wall, often at the entrance or registration. Stage branding is built into the stage itself and has to fit around screens, lighting, cabling and speakers. It is produced to the production company's plan and usually installed during the venue's load-in window alongside the AV team.

Which materials work best under stage lighting?

Matte fabrics and matte-laminated foamex handle lights and cameras well. Gloss vinyl and glossy laminates reflect spotlights as bright patches, especially on large areas like fascias. Very pale backgrounds can also flare on camera, so mid-tones and darker colours often look better on screen than pure white.

Can you work to our production company's schedule?

Yes. Share the stage plan, the list of graphics and the load-in time as early as possible, and put us in touch with the production manager. How long production takes depends on the number of pieces and materials involved, so we agree dates with you when we quote and plan so graphics are packed and ready for the load-in.
