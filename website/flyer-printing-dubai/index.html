About flyer printing

Flyer printing in Dubai remains one of the most direct ways to put an offer in someone's hand. Flyers are handed out at events and store openings, left at residential doors, tucked into delivery bags and stacked on reception counters. Because each sheet carries one message, they work best when the offer, the call to action and the contact method are obvious at a glance.

We print flyers and leaflets as flat single sheets. A5 (148 × 210 mm) is the everyday size for promotions; DL (99 × 210 mm) fits envelopes and menu holders; A6 suits bag inserts and vouchers; A4 gives room for service lists and detailed information. A single fold turns an A4 sheet into a simple four-panel leaflet. For multi-panel or stapled documents, our brochure range is the better fit.

Paper choice changes how a flyer is received. A 130 gsm sheet is light and suits large-volume drops, 170 gsm feels more substantial, and 300–350 gsm card flyers survive pockets and notice boards. Gloss suits food and product photography; silk and matt read more comfortably for text-heavy layouts.

Materials

130–150 gsm gloss

Light and bright, with vivid colour. Suited to high-volume door drops and bag inserts where weight matters.

170 gsm silk

A smoother, low-sheen surface that feels more substantial in the hand and handles both photos and text well.

300–350 gsm card

Thick enough to stand in a holder or survive a pocket, used for vouchers, event programmes and counter handouts.

Uncoated 120–150 gsm

Easy to write on and free of glare, useful for coupons with handwritten details or a more natural brand look.

Finishing options

Matt or gloss lamination

Added to card flyers that will be handled repeatedly, protecting the print from scuffs and moisture.

Spot UV

Clear gloss highlights on a laminated card flyer, making an offer or logo catch the light.

Perforated tear-off

A perforated strip creates a detachable coupon or reply slip without scissors.

Scoring and folding

Heavier sheets are creased before folding so the fold stays sharp and the ink does not crack.

Artwork checklist

  • Supply a PDF at final size with 3 mm bleed and crop marks
  • Keep text at least 4 mm from the trim edge
  • Use images at 300 dpi at the printed size
  • Use rich black (for example 40C 30M 30Y 100K) for large dark areas, and 100% black only for small text
  • Place QR codes at 20 mm or larger and test them from the printed proof
  • Put the offer and call to action in the top third, where the eye lands first

Flyer printing FAQs

Which flyer size should I choose?

A5 is the most versatile size for promotions and handouts. A6 is compact and suits vouchers, inserts and counter stacks. DL fits standard envelopes and menu holders and works well for restaurant menus. A4 gives space for detailed service lists or property information. If you are unsure, consider how the flyer will be carried, displayed or posted.

What is the difference between a flyer and a leaflet?

In practice the terms overlap. Flyers are usually single sheets designed for wide distribution and one clear message, while leaflets tend to carry a little more information and may have a single fold. Both are printed from the same range of papers. Once you need three or more panels or stapled pages, a brochure is the better format.

Gloss or matt paper for flyers?

Gloss makes food, product and property photography look bright and saturated, and suits most promotional flyers. Matt and silk papers reduce glare, feel more refined and are easier to read when there is a lot of text. If people need to write on the flyer, choose an uncoated paper, since coated surfaces can smudge ballpoint ink.

Can you print different versions for different branches?

Yes. Digital flyer printing makes it practical to change branch addresses, phone numbers, offer codes or languages within a single order. Each version is proofed separately and packed in labelled bundles so they reach the right location. It is also a simple way to compare response between two designs.

Do you distribute flyers?

We focus on design, printing and finishing, and supply flyers packed and labelled for your own team or distribution agency. If flyers are going into letterboxes, envelopes or delivery bags, tell us so we can recommend a suitable weight and size. Check with your community or building management about local rules on door drops.
