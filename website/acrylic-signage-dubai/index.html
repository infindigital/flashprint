About acrylic signage

Acrylic signage in Dubai has become the default for interior signs that need to look finished rather than temporary. Cast acrylic sheet takes a crisp laser-cut edge, can be flame- or diamond-polished to a clear finish, and accepts full-colour UV printing directly on its surface. The result sits comfortably beside glass partitions, stone counters and timber joinery.

The most popular construction is second-surface printing. Artwork is printed in reverse on the back of clear acrylic, backed with a white or colour flood, and viewed through the sheet. The print is shielded from cleaning and fingerprints while the front keeps its glossy depth. For a layered look, a clear face panel can be stood off a coloured or mirrored back panel, with lettering on either layer.

Our acrylic signage runs from 200 × 75 mm door signs to large directory boards and shaped pieces cut to a logo outline. Tell us where the sign will hang, how far away it will be read and what the wall is made of, and we will recommend thickness, print method and fixings.

Materials

Clear cast acrylic

The standard for reverse-printed signs and layered panels. Its clarity shows off polished edges and stand-off fixings.

Frosted (satin) acrylic

A soft matt surface that hides fingerprints and gives a sandblasted-glass look, well suited to meeting room signs and wayfinding plates.

Opal acrylic

Diffuses light evenly, so it is the face material of choice for backlit panels and edge-lit signs.

Coloured and mirror acrylic

Solid colours carry brand colour right through the edge. Mirror gold, silver or rose-gold suits hospitality and retail, but scratches more readily, so keep it away from high-touch areas.

Finishing options

Second-surface print

Printed on the back and read through the sheet, keeping the image protected and the face glossy.

Face print with matt varnish

Printed on the front for a flat, glare-free look under strong office lighting.

Cut vinyl lettering

Plotted vinyl on the face or reverse; practical for text that may be updated later.

Engraving with paint fill

Laser-engraved text filled with colour for a tactile, permanent finish on door plates.

Edge lighting

LED strips in a base or top channel make engraved graphics in clear acrylic glow.

Why choose us for acrylic signage

  • Edges finished properly

    Every cut edge is polished or cleanly lasered, so signs look deliberate even when viewed up close.

  • Thickness matched to size

    Thin sheet for door plates, thicker sheet where stand-offs must hold a large panel flat between fixings.

  • Print and cut aligned

    Printed graphics and cut outlines come from the same file, so shaped signs keep an even border all round.

  • Fitted level and square

    Positions are marked with a level and drilled to a template, keeping rows of door signs at one consistent height.

Artwork checklist

  • Supply vector artwork (AI, EPS or PDF with outlined fonts) for any shape that will be laser-cut
  • Draw the cut line as a separate spot-colour layer and keep text at least 10 mm from any cut edge
  • Send reverse-print artwork the right way round; we mirror it for production
  • Mark which areas should stay clear, which are white-backed and which are colour-filled
  • Leave room for stand-off holes, usually 25–40 mm in from each corner, when placing logos and text
  • Raster images for printed panels should be 150–300 dpi at final size

Acrylic signage FAQs

Is acrylic better than glass for indoor signs?

For most interior signs, yes. Acrylic is about half the weight of glass, much less likely to shatter and can be laser-cut into shapes that are difficult to achieve in glass. Glass has a harder surface that resists scratching, so it still suits some high-end reception features. For door signs, directories and information panels, acrylic gives a similar look with easier handling and fixing.

What thickness of acrylic should I choose?

Door and room signs usually use 3–5 mm. Wall panels on stand-offs look and perform better at 5–8 mm, because thicker sheet stays flat between fixings. Freestanding desk signs and logo blocks go to 10 mm or more. We recommend a thickness based on panel size, fixing method and the edge you want to see.

How do I clean acrylic signs without scratching them?

Use a soft microfibre cloth with mild soapy water or a cleaner made for acrylic. Avoid ammonia-based glass cleaners, alcohol wipes and paper towels, which can cloud or scratch the surface over time. Reverse-printed signs are the easiest to maintain because the print sits behind the sheet and cleaning only touches plain acrylic.

Can acrylic signs be updated when names change?

Yes, in a few ways. Names can be cut vinyl on the face, removed and replaced without new acrylic. A two-layer sign with a clear slide-in pocket takes printed inserts your own team can change. For fully printed signs, we reprint a single panel from the original artwork so it matches the rest of the set.

Can acrylic signs be fixed to glass partitions?

They can. Small panels bond well to glass with clear VHB tape, and the adhesive can be hidden behind a printed or frosted area. Through-glass stand-offs are possible on some partitions but must be planned before the glass is made, because toughened glass cannot be drilled after tempering. We check the partition type during the survey.
