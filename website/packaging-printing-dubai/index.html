Packaging printing in Dubai is where a brand is held, carried and opened, so every piece has to work structurally as well as look right. For retailers, restaurants, cosmetic and fragrance brands, hotels and corporate gifting teams, we print the parts that make up a pack: custom boxes, laminated paper bags, kraft bags, product cartons and sleeves, and the tags and seals that finish them.

Every project starts with the product itself: its size, its weight, how it travels and where it is sold. From there we recommend a structure and board, set up the dieline, check your artwork against it and send a proof, with a physical sample where the structure is new. Timelines depend on quantity, construction and finishing, and we confirm the schedule with your quote before production.

Boxes, bags or a full packaging system

The five services in this category overlap, so it helps to know where each one starts and stops. Many brands use several together, for example a printed carton inside a kraft mailer, finished with a foil seal and a swing tag, then handed over in a laminated carrier bag.

A simple rule of thumb helps. If the product needs protecting or presenting, start with a box. If it needs carrying out of the shop or restaurant, start with a bag, laminated for presentation or kraft for everyday volume. If several products must look like one family, plan product packaging. And if the pack is right but feels unfinished, tags and seals are usually the answer.

  • Custom boxes: the box structure itself, from folding tuck-end cartons and corrugated mailers to rigid lid-and-base, drawer and magnetic-closure gift boxes.
  • Product packaging: the complete retail pack for a product range, combining cartons, sleeves, inserts and labels across several SKUs with consistent branding and information panels.
  • Paper bags: laminated art-paper carrier bags with rope or ribbon handles, for boutiques, perfume counters, events, hotels and corporate gifts.
  • Kraft bags: brown or white kraft bags with twisted, flat or no handles, for takeaway, bakery, grocery and everyday retail.
  • Labels and tags: swing tags, hang tags, box seals and belly bands that complete a pack and carry price, size, care or product details.

Board, paper and finish options

Folding cartons are usually printed on solid bleached board (SBS) or folding boxboard between 300 and 400 gsm, with kraft board for a natural look. Rigid boxes wrap printed paper around 1.5–2.5 mm greyboard, which gives the weight and solid feel expected of a gift or luxury box. Shipping and subscription boxes use corrugated board, most often E-flute, which is thin enough to print well yet strong enough for courier handling.

Bags follow a similar split. Coated art paper or card of around 157–250 gsm, laminated and reinforced at the top and base, makes a premium carrier bag, while 80–150 gsm kraft suits takeaway and everyday bags that are used in volume.

Finishes then carry the brand. Matte or soft-touch lamination adds feel and protects print, hot foil adds metallic logos, spot UV creates gloss contrast on matte surfaces, and embossing or debossing gives a logo you can feel. Used with restraint, one or two finishes often look more premium than four.

How a packaging project runs

Packaging involves more decisions than flat print, so we work through it in stages and show you each one before committing to production.

  • Brief: product dimensions, weight, quantity, how it ships and where it is sold.
  • Structure: we suggest a style and board, then supply a dieline for your designer or ours.
  • Artwork check: bleed, glue flaps, fold lines, barcodes and finishing layers are checked against the dieline.
  • Proof and sample: a digital proof, plus a plain or printed sample for new structures where needed.
  • Production and finishing: printing, lamination, foil or spot UV, die-cutting, gluing and assembly.
  • Quality check and delivery: packed flat or assembled, as agreed in your quote.

FAQs

Packaging Printing FAQs

Can you create a dieline for our product?

Yes. Send the product dimensions, or a sample if you can, and tell us how it will be sold and shipped. We recommend a box style and board, then prepare a dieline your designer can work on, or our design team can lay out the artwork for you. For new structures we can make a sample to check the fit before printing.

What is the difference between a folding carton and a rigid box?

A folding carton is printed on a single layer of board, die-cut and supplied flat, then folded into shape when packed, which makes it light and space-saving. A rigid box is made from thick greyboard wrapped in printed paper and is usually supplied assembled. Rigid boxes feel heavier and more substantial, which is why they are used for gifts and luxury products.

Can you print packaging for food products?

We print a wide range of food packaging, including cake and pastry boxes, date and chocolate boxes, takeaway bags and product labels. If the packaging will be in direct contact with unwrapped food, tell us at the brief stage so the board, inks and coatings can be specified with that in mind. Many food brands also use a greaseproof liner or an inner bag.

Do you print small packaging runs for new brands?

Yes. Short runs are often printed digitally, which suits product launches, samples and seasonal editions, while larger volumes usually move to offset printing for consistency across big runs. Tell us your quantities and how you expect them to grow, and we will recommend the most practical route and confirm the details in your quote.
