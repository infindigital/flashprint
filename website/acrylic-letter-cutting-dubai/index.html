About acrylic letter cutting

Acrylic letter cutting in Dubai is the usual way to build a crisp, dimensional logo for a reception or office wall without the depth of a fabricated box letter. The laser follows the vector outline of each character, giving sharp corners and a glossy edge on clear and coloured acrylic, so letters look finished as soon as they come off the machine.

Thickness sets the presence of the sign. Letters of 3–5 mm suit door signs and small wall graphics, 8–10 mm is the usual choice for reception logos, and 15–20 mm gives heavier, sculptural letters that cast a clear shadow when mounted on stand-offs. Tall letters in thin acrylic can flex, so we suggest a thicker sheet or a backing plate when the size calls for it.

Colour can come from the acrylic itself, from vinyl or a printed face applied before cutting, or from paint on the back of clear acrylic for a glossy, glass-like front. Mirror gold and silver acrylic suit hospitality and beauty brands. For illuminated signs, cut acrylic faces and letters are combined with LED lighting as part of our 3D signage work.

Materials

Cast acrylic

Higher clarity and a cleaner polished cut edge. The preferred sheet for thick letters and for engraving.

Extruded acrylic

Very consistent thickness, well suited to thinner letters and larger quantities of simple shapes.

Mirror acrylic

Reflective gold, silver or rose-gold face on an acrylic base. The protective film stays on until installation to avoid scratches.

Opal and frosted acrylic

Diffuses light evenly, which makes it the choice for backlit letters and soft, glass-like panels.

Finishing options

Polished laser edge

Clear, glossy cut edge that needs no extra finishing on most acrylics.

Reverse painted

Paint on the back of clear acrylic so the front stays glossy with colour showing through.

Vinyl or printed face

Brand colours or a multi-colour logo applied to the face before cutting.

Stand-off mounting

Letters or panels held off the wall on metal pins to create shadow and depth.

Acrylic letter cutting FAQs

What thickness of acrylic should we use for reception letters?

For most reception walls, 8–10 mm gives letters enough presence without looking heavy. Smaller text and door signage work at 3–5 mm. Large logos, or walls viewed from across a lobby, benefit from 15–20 mm or letters on stand-off pins, which add shadow and depth. We suggest a thickness once we know the letter height and the viewing distance.

How are acrylic letters fixed to the wall?

Small letters are usually fixed flush with high-bond tape or adhesive, using a paper template for spacing. Larger letters can be mounted on stand-off pins or locating studs drilled into the wall, which also lets them sit off the surface for a shadow effect. Letters can also be fixed to a clear or coloured backing panel that is then mounted as one piece.

Can acrylic letters be used outdoors?

Acrylic itself copes with sun well, but outdoor use needs the right adhesives, fixings and colour finishes, and thin letters can move with heat on sun-facing walls. For exposed shop fronts and facades, fabricated 3D letters or ACP-backed signs are often the better choice. Tell us where the sign will go and we will recommend a build.

Can you match our brand colour in acrylic?

Solid acrylic comes in a set range of colours, so a close brand match is often achieved another way: a vinyl or printed face on the acrylic, or reverse-painted clear acrylic mixed to your Pantone reference. Send the colour code and we will show which option gets closest, then confirm it on the proof.
