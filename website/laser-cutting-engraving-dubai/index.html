Laser cutting and engraving in Dubai serves two different needs. Cutting follows a vector path to separate a shape from a sheet, which is how acrylic letters, MDF display parts and decorative panels are made. Engraving marks or removes a thin layer of the surface, leaving text, logos or patterns that are part of the material rather than printed on top of it.

We work from your artwork on common sheet materials and finished items: cast and extruded acrylic, MDF, plywood, solid wood and bamboo, coated metals and stainless steel. Many jobs combine processes, such as laser-cut acrylic letters with a printed face, or a wooden plaque carrying an engraved brass plate. Tell us what the finished piece should look like and where it will be used, and we will suggest the material and method.

Cutting versus engraving: what each process does

A CO2 laser cuts acrylic, wood and MDF along the exact outline in your file. On clear and coloured acrylic the cut edge comes out glossy, so letters and shapes usually need no further edge finishing. On wood and MDF the heat darkens the edge, which some designs keep as a feature and others paint over. Because nothing touches the sheet except the beam, small and intricate shapes are cut without clamp marks or blade wear.

Engraving works in two ways. Raster engraving scans back and forth to fill areas such as logos and blocks of text; vector engraving traces fine lines for outlines, borders and hairline detail. Coated metals, anodised aluminium and painted surfaces are engraved by removing the coating to reveal the metal beneath, while bare stainless steel and brass need a marking method suited to metal, which we confirm for your item.

Materials and typical thicknesses

Thickness decides both what can be cut cleanly in one pass and how solid the finished piece feels. These are common working ranges; exact options are confirmed in your quote.

  • Acrylic: commonly 3–20 mm in clear, opal, frosted, solid colours, mirror and fluorescent sheet
  • MDF: commonly 3–12 mm, raw or pre-finished, for display parts, signage bases and templates
  • Plywood and solid wood: thin sheets for cut shapes; boards and finished items for engraving
  • Metals: stainless steel, anodised aluminium, brass and coated plates for engraving and marking
  • Leatherette, cork and card: tags, coasters, menu covers and packaging details

Planning a laser job

Laser work starts from vector artwork. Cut lines need to be single closed paths, and text should be converted to outlines so the font cannot change between computers. For engraving, a one-colour logo works best, because the laser produces a single tone set by the material rather than by ink colours.

Tell us the finished size, the material and thickness, the quantity and how the piece will be fixed or displayed. We check the file, send a proof showing sizes and cut paths, and confirm the schedule with the quote once artwork is ready. Timelines depend on quantity, material and finishing.

  • Finished size of each piece and the quantity
  • Material, colour and thickness, or the look you are after
  • Fixing method, stand or backing panel
  • Any printed, painted or illuminated elements

FAQs

Laser Cutting & Engraving FAQs

What file do you need for laser cutting or engraving?

Vector files such as AI, EPS, PDF, SVG or DXF work best. Cut paths should be closed outlines, text should be converted to curves, and engraving areas should be solid fills in one colour. If you only have a JPG or a sketch, our design team can redraw it as a vector file before cutting.

Can laser engraving be done in colour?

Not directly. The laser removes or marks the material, so the result is one tone: frosted white on clear acrylic, dark brown on wood, silver on anodised aluminium. Colour can be added by filling engraved areas with paint, by layering coloured or mirror acrylic, or by combining laser-cut parts with a printed face.

Can you engrave items we supply?

Often, yes, if the material is suitable and the item fits the machine and can be held flat or steady. We check the item first and, where practical, test on a hidden area or a spare. Some plastics and coatings are not suited to laser work, so send a photo and material details before bringing items in.

How thick can you cut acrylic and MDF?

Common working ranges are about 3–20 mm for acrylic and 3–12 mm for MDF, with thinner sheet used for fine detail and small parts. Thicker pieces can often be built up by laminating layers together. Tell us the look and size you want, and we will confirm the right thickness for the design.
