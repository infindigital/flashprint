About t-shirt printing

T-shirt printing in Dubai usually starts with an event date and a head count. The questions that follow decide the method: how many colours the design has, how many shirts you need, which fabric suits the setting, and whether any shirts carry individual names or numbers.

Screen printing lays down thick, opaque ink one colour at a time, giving strong colour on dark and light cotton, and it suits larger runs of a simple logo. DTF prints the whole design onto film, gradients and photographs included, then heat-presses it onto the shirt, so it is practical for smaller quantities and multi-colour artwork. Heat transfer vinyl is cut from sheet vinyl and is the neat way to add names, numbers or a one-colour logo.

For outdoor events in the UAE heat, a lightweight polyester or performance tee is more comfortable than heavy cotton, and sublimation can be used on white polyester for all-over designs. For merchandise that people will keep and wear, a heavier combed cotton shirt with a soft-feel print comes closer to a retail garment.

Materials

Combed cotton

Soft, smooth and breathable, with a tight surface that holds fine print detail. The safe default for merchandise and staff tees.

Polyester performance

Lightweight, moisture-wicking fabric for sports days, marathons and outdoor activations. Pairs with DTF or sublimation rather than plastisol.

Poly-cotton blend

Holds its shape and shrinks less than pure cotton. A sensible middle ground for event shirts that will be worn again.

Heavyweight cotton

Thicker, structured shirts from around 220 gsm upwards with a substantial hand-feel, suited to brand and café merchandise.

Finishing options

Screen print

Vivid, opaque spot colours with a smooth surface. Best for bold logos repeated across a larger run.

DTF transfer

Full colour with fine detail and gradients on almost any fabric, with a flexible, thin feel.

Heat transfer vinyl

Crisp one-colour cut graphics, names and numbers in matte, gloss, metallic or reflective vinyl.

Sublimation

Edge-to-edge colour on white polyester that becomes part of the fabric, with no surface feel at all.

Artwork checklist

  • Send vector artwork (AI, EPS, PDF) or a PNG at 300 dpi at the final print size
  • For DTF, use a transparent background, as any non-transparent area will print
  • For screen printing, list Pantone colours and keep the colour count to what the design needs
  • Keep fine lines above about 0.5 mm and small text bold enough to read on fabric
  • On dark shirts a white base layer sits under the colours, so check how your logo looks on dark

T-shirt printing FAQs

What is the difference between screen printing and DTF?

Screen printing pushes ink through a separate mesh screen for each colour, so set-up takes longer but large runs of a simple design print with rich, opaque colour. DTF prints the full design onto film in one pass and heat-presses it on, so there is no set-up per colour. That makes DTF the better fit for small quantities, photographs and designs with many colours.

Which t-shirt fabric is best for outdoor events in Dubai?

For daytime outdoor events, lightweight polyester or a poly-cotton blend is usually more comfortable than heavy cotton, because it wicks sweat and dries faster in the heat. Pale colours stay cooler in direct sun. Performance fabrics pair best with DTF, heat transfer or sublimation rather than thick plastisol screen inks.

Can we mix sizes and colours in one order?

Yes. Most orders mix sizes, and many mix shirt colours too, for example staff in black and volunteers in white with the same print. Send a breakdown per colour and size in a simple table. We confirm stock for each combination in the quote, since popular sizes in some colours can run low.

Can you print names and numbers on each shirt?

Yes. Individual names, numbers or roles are usually applied in heat transfer vinyl or DTF on the back, with the main design printed across the full run. Send a spreadsheet listing each name, number and size together. We proof the layout and the full list before production so spelling can be checked.
