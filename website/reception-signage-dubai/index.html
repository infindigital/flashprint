About reception signage

Reception signage in Dubai sets the tone for an office the moment a visitor arrives. It is usually the largest interior sign in the fit-out and the one most likely to appear in team photos, video calls and social posts, so its proportions, lighting and finish deserve more attention than any door plate.

Most reception features are built from individual letters or a logo cut from 10–20 mm acrylic, brushed stainless steel or aluminium, pinned to the wall on concealed studs so they float 15–30 mm off the surface. A backing panel in timber veneer, painted MDF, fluted cladding or aluminium composite can frame the logo when the wall itself is not suitable. Halo lighting, where LEDs behind each letter wash light onto the wall, gives a soft glow that suits hotel lobbies and executive floors.

Size is set by the wall and the viewing position. We work from a photo and measurements of your reception area, place the logo on it at scale, and agree height, width and centre line with you before fabrication starts.

Specifications

Logo sizes
Commonly 1,200–3,000 mm wide; strapline letters from around 100 mm high
Letter materials
10–20 mm acrylic, brushed or mirror stainless steel, aluminium, or acrylic with a metal-finish face
Backing
Direct to wall, or on a timber, painted MDF, ACP or acrylic feature panel
Illumination
Halo-lit or face-lit LED on request; needs a power point and driver location
Fixing
Threaded studs into the wall at a 15–30 mm offset, set out with a full-size template
Extras
Desk-front logos, straplines and glass graphics to match
Specifications shown are common options. Final sizes, materials, finishes and quantities are confirmed in your quote.

Materials

Solid colour acrylic

Even colour through the full thickness, with matt or gloss faces. Black and white are the most requested, followed by exact brand colours.

Brushed stainless steel

A calm corporate look. Fabricated letters with folded returns give real depth on larger logos.

Mirror metal or mirror acrylic

Reflects light and colour from the room, which is striking in hotel lobbies, though it shows fingerprints and belongs above hand height.

Timber and fluted feature panels

Warm backdrops that frame metal or acrylic letters, often matched to the reception desk joinery.

Finishing options

Halo lighting

LEDs behind each letter light the wall around it, creating a soft outline rather than a bright face.

Face-lit

Opal acrylic faces glow evenly, keeping the logo legible in a darker lobby.

Painted returns

Letter sides in a contrast colour add depth when the logo is seen from an angle.

Brushed or satin metal

Directional grain gives a low-glare finish under ceiling downlights.

Where it’s used

  • Logo wall behind the reception desk
  • Front panel of the reception counter
  • Lift lobby logos on multi-floor offices
  • Clinic and dental reception features
  • Hotel and serviced-apartment lobbies
  • Showroom and sales centre welcome walls

Industries we support with reception signage: Hotels & Hospitality, Healthcare & Clinics, Real Estate, Retail.

Artwork checklist

  • Supply the logo as vector artwork (AI, EPS or PDF) with fonts converted to outlines
  • Strokes must be wide enough to take a fixing stud, usually at least 10–12 mm; thinner details can be printed onto a backing panel instead
  • Share a straight-on photo of the wall with its measured width and the desk height
  • Specify brand colours as Pantone or RAL references for paint and acrylic matching
  • Tell us if the logo also has a single-colour version for metal or engraved use

Reception signage FAQs

How big should my reception logo be?

A useful guide is to fill roughly half to two-thirds of the wall width above the desk, leaving clear space either side. The logo should also read comfortably from the entrance door, not only from the desk. We place it on a photo of your wall at two or three sizes so you can compare before committing.

What does halo lighting need on site?

Halo-lit letters need a nearby power point, a location for the LED driver and a route for low-voltage cables behind the wall or panel. Ideally this is agreed before the wall is finished so cables can be concealed. On existing walls, a backing panel can hide the wiring. We coordinate these details with your fit-out contractor.

Can the logo go on glass, stone or a fluted wall?

Yes, with the right method. On glass, lightweight acrylic letters can be bonded with clear adhesive, since toughened glass cannot be drilled once installed. On stone, veneer or fluted panels, we pin or bond letters depending on the substrate. Where a surface should not be drilled at all, a backing panel fixed at a few points carries the letters.

Is metal or acrylic better for reception letters?

Acrylic suits colourful logos, crisp flat faces and detailed shapes, and it is lighter to fix. Brushed or mirror stainless steel reads as more solid and upmarket, especially on larger letters with deep returns. Many clients combine them, and metal-faced acrylic gives a metallic look on detailed lettering that would be hard to fabricate in solid metal.

Can you move our reception sign to a new office?

Often, yes. Stud-mounted letters can be taken down, cleaned and refitted using a new template, provided they come off undamaged. Bonded letters are harder to recover. We inspect the sign before removal and tell you plainly whether reinstalling or remaking is the better option.
