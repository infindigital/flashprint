Partial Vehicle Wraps and Half Wraps

A partial wrap covers selected panels, such as the rear half, the sides or the tailgate, and uses the vehicle's own paint colour as part of the design. It gives vans, pick-ups and company cars clear branding from the angles that matter while covering only part of the body.

Typical coverage
Quarter, half or three-quarter wrap
Design approach
Graphics fade into the paint colour
Film
Cast or polymeric, chosen per panel

About partial vehicle wrapping

Partial vehicle wrapping in Dubai is the practical middle ground between door logos and a full wrap. Instead of covering every panel, the design concentrates on the areas people actually read: the rear doors of a van in traffic, the side panels in a car park, the tailgate of a pick-up at a site gate. The rest of the body stays in its original paint.

The design has to work with that paint. A white van suits graphics that sweep in from the rear and fade to white by the front doors; a silver or grey car needs the cut-off line placed along a natural body crease so the join looks deliberate. We check the paint colour against the proof so any background colour in the print blends rather than clashes.

Because partial wraps often sit on flatter areas, film choice can be adjusted. The sides of box vans and panel vans with few curves can take polymeric calendered film, while bumpers, arches and rounded tailgates still need cast film. We specify panel by panel and explain the reasoning in the quote.

Materials

Cast wrap vinyl

Used wherever the graphic crosses an arch, bumper or rounded tailgate. It follows the shape without lifting and stays put when the panel gets hot.

Polymeric calendered vinyl

A slightly thicker, stiffer film that performs well on flat van sides and box bodies. It suits partial jobs where the graphic stays on large flat areas.

Cut vinyl lettering

Solid-colour vinyl cut to shape for phone numbers, websites and small text, added on top of or beside the printed panels.

Perforated window film

Carries the design across the rear glass when the graphic reaches the back of the vehicle, while the driver keeps a view out.

Finishing options

Gloss laminate

The closest match to factory paint, so the printed area sits naturally beside the unwrapped panels.

Satin laminate

A lower sheen that suits darker vehicles and reduces reflections on large photographic areas.

Matte laminate

For deliberately flat, modern designs. It will look different from the glossy paint next to it, which can be part of the effect.

Where it’s used

  • Service vans for technicians and maintenance teams
  • Contractor pick-ups used on site
  • Rear-door advertising on delivery vans
  • Side-panel branding on company cars
  • Promotional vehicles on short campaigns

Industries we support with partial vehicle wrapping: Construction, Retail, Restaurants & Cafés, Healthcare & Clinics.

Why choose us for partial vehicle wrapping

  • Designed around your paint

    Fades and cut-off lines are planned so print and paint read as one design, not a sticker on a car.

  • Film chosen per panel

    Flat and curved panels are specified separately, so each part of the graphic uses film suited to its shape.

  • Joins placed on body lines

    Edges finish along creases, trim and panel gaps, where they are least visible and least likely to catch.

  • Easy to extend later

    The artwork can grow into a full wrap or be adapted to more vehicles without starting again.

Artwork checklist

  • Tell us the paint colour name or code and send daylight photos of the vehicle
  • Mark on a photo roughly where you want the graphic to start and finish
  • Supply vector logos (AI, EPS or PDF) with fonts outlined
  • Keep contact details on flat areas above the wheel-arch line, not across handles
  • Let the design fade into the paint colour rather than ending in a hard box shape

Partial vehicle wrapping FAQs

What is the difference between a partial wrap and a full wrap?

A full wrap covers every painted panel and replaces the look of the vehicle entirely. A partial wrap covers selected areas, often a half or three-quarter of the body, and leaves the remaining paint visible as part of the design. It uses less film and less installation time while still delivering large, readable graphics.

Will the printed colour match my vehicle's paint?

We can get close, but printed ink on vinyl and automotive paint are different materials, so an exact match under every light is unrealistic. The usual approach is to let the graphic fade to white or finish on a body line, where a slight difference is not noticeable. We show you the proposed join on the proof before printing.

Which panels give the most visibility?

On vans, the rear doors and both sides above the wheel arches are read most often, in traffic and when parked. On pick-ups the tailgate and rear quarters matter most. Cars are mostly seen side-on, so rear doors and quarter panels come first. We suggest coverage based on how and where each vehicle is used.

Is a partial wrap suitable for a dark vehicle?

It is, with some planning. Wrap film has a white base, so a design meant to fade into black or navy paint needs a printed background that matches the vehicle colour, or a clean edge along a body line. White and light graphics read very well against dark paint, which often makes the simplest designs the most effective.
