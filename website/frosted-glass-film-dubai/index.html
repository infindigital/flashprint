About frosted glass film

Frosted glass film in Dubai offices solves a familiar fit-out problem. Glass partitions look open and modern, but nobody wants to hold a confidential meeting in a fish tank. A film applied to the glass diffuses the view while keeping the light, and involves far less disruption than replacing panels with sandblasted glass.

The simplest option is full-height frosting across the pane. Many offices prefer a band between roughly 900 and 1,800 mm from the floor, which blocks seated and standing sightlines while leaving the top and bottom clear. Logos, patterns and room names can be cut out of the frost so they show as clear glass, or cut from frost and applied to clear glass for the opposite effect. Printed frosted film adds colour and gradients for a more designed partition.

Glass walls also need to be noticed by people walking towards them. Frosted bands, dots or lines at the heights your fit-out consultant or building specifies do this job neatly and can carry your brand at the same time.

Specifications

Film types
Standard frost, dusted crystal, white matt, coloured translucent and printed frosted film
Roll widths
Commonly 1,220 and 1,520 mm; wider panes are joined on a neat vertical line
Formats
Full coverage, horizontal bands, stripes, gradients, cut logos and patterns
Cutting
Plotter-cut designs, with fine internal details weeded by hand
Application
Wet-applied with slip solution and squeegee, trimmed to the frame on site
Removal
Peels off with heat and a scraper, leaving glass ready for new film
Specifications shown are common options. Final sizes, materials, finishes and quantities are confirmed in your quote.

Materials

Standard frosted film

The classic etched-glass look, even and neutral, suitable for most offices and clinics.

Dusted crystal film

A finer, slightly brighter frost that closely resembles sandblasting, often chosen for executive floors and hotels.

Printed frosted film

Colour, patterns or gradient fades printed onto a frosted base for decorative partitions.

Coloured translucent film

Tints glass in brand colours while still passing light, used for accent panels in creative workplaces.

Finishing options

Cut-out logo

The logo is cut away so it reads as clear glass within the frost.

Reverse cut

Logo or lettering cut from frost and applied to clear glass, for a lighter touch.

Gradient fade

A printed dot or tone fade from solid frost to clear that softens the edge of a band.

Stripes and patterns

Horizontal lines or geometric motifs that give partial privacy and visual interest.

Where it’s used

  • Meeting room and boardroom partitions
  • Private offices along glass corridors
  • Clinic consultation and treatment rooms
  • Hotel bathroom and spa glass
  • Ground-floor windows facing the street
  • Glass doors that need visibility markings

Industries we support with frosted glass film: Healthcare & Clinics, Hotels & Hospitality, Real Estate, Schools & Education.

Why choose us for frosted glass film

  • Measured pane by pane

    Every pane is measured individually, since partitions that look the same often vary slightly.

  • Clean edges at frames

    Film is trimmed in place to an even margin from the frame or gasket, giving a factory-frosted appearance.

  • Bands set to sightlines

    We check seated and standing eye levels in the actual room before fixing band heights.

  • Branding built in

    Logos and patterns cut into the frost act as manifestation and decoration at once.

Artwork checklist

  • Provide glass sizes for each pane, or let us measure on site for larger jobs
  • Supply logos as vector files with clean, closed outlines, because frost is cut rather than printed
  • Avoid lines thinner than about 2 mm, which are hard to weed and apply
  • State band heights from finished floor level and which side of the glass faces the corridor
  • For printed frosted film, supply artwork at 1:1 with 10 mm bleed on every edge

No print-ready file? Our file setup and design team can prepare it.

Frosted glass film FAQs

Does frosted film block all visibility?

It blurs shapes and detail rather than blacking out the glass. People on the other side appear as soft silhouettes, especially when the room behind is brightly lit. For stronger privacy, choose a denser frost or white matt film, or combine a frosted band with blinds in rooms that need full screening in the evening.

Which side of the glass is the film applied to?

Usually the side that is less exposed to knocks and has fewer fittings in the way. On internal partitions both sides look much the same, so we choose the one with better access. On exterior windows the film goes inside, protected from dust and weather. We confirm the side for each pane before installation.

Can frosted film be removed later?

Yes. The film peels off with a little heat and a scraper, and any adhesive left behind is cleaned with a suitable remover. The glass underneath is ready for new film or can be left clear. That makes frosting a sensible choice for leased offices where the landlord expects clear glass at the end of the tenancy.

Can you frost glass doors with handles and locks?

Yes. The film is cut around handles, locks, hinges and patch fittings on site so it sits neatly around the hardware. On frameless doors we leave a small margin at the edges so the film does not catch or lift as the door swings. Push and pull text can be cut into the frost at the same time.

Is frosted film suitable for bathroom and shower glass?

It works on hotel and residential bathroom glass when a film suited to humid rooms is used and applied to the dry side where possible. Constant water on the film surface can lift its edges over time. For shower screens, we assess the layout and advise whether film or another option is more practical.
